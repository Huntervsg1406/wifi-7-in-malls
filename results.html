Results & Findings

Results from the WiFi 7 field investigation conducted at Westfield Newmarket.

Experiment Results

LV4M-01

Low Crowd Density

This experiment was conducted in an open section of the mall rooftop with a low crowd density of approximately 10–12 people. The location provided minimal human obstruction between the router and mobile device and served as the baseline test condition.

Observation

At low crowd density, 2.4 GHz produced the strongest average RSSI at -28.8 dBm, while 5 GHz UDP experienced notable packet loss of 20% despite adequate signal strength, suggesting early signs of interference even at low occupancy.

LV1-04

Medium Crowd Density

This experiment was conducted at a second mall location under medium crowd density conditions. The increased number of people introduced additional signal obstructions and greater multipath propagation.

Observation

Medium crowd density resulted in a noticeable drop in RSSI across all bands compared to LV4M-01, with average values declining by approximately 30 dBm.

Despite weaker signal strength, 6 GHz TCP achieved the highest throughput at 105 Mbps, suggesting that channel width advantages at 6 GHz compensated for signal loss at medium density. UDP packet loss remained relatively low across all bands at this location.

LV3-03

Heavy Crowd Density

This experiment was conducted under heavy crowd density conditions and represented the most challenging propagation environment in the study.

The high number of people present created significant signal absorption, increased multipath reflections and elevated co-channel interference.

Observation

Heavy crowd density produced the weakest RSSI readings across the tests. 6 GHz UDP averaged -84.45 dBm and 5 GHz TCP reached -84.37 dBm.

High packet loss was observed on 2.4 GHz UDP and 6 GHz UDP, with both recording approximately 55% packet loss.

MLO recorded the strongest average RSSI at -34.64 dBm despite the heavy density conditions.

LV2-06

Heavy Crowd Density

This experiment represented a second heavy density testing location and was intended to assess the consistency of results across different areas under similar crowd conditions.

Observation

The available 2.4 GHz UDP result showed a stronger average RSSI of -29 dBm compared with LV3-03 under similar crowd density.

This suggests that the physical layout of the location provided better propagation conditions.

Overall Findings

Crowd Density

As crowd density increased from low to medium conditions, average RSSI declined considerably. The higher frequency bands experienced particularly significant degradation under heavy crowd conditions.

The results also showed that crowd density alone did not determine performance. LV2-06 produced a much stronger 2.4 GHz RSSI than LV3-03 despite both being heavy-density locations, indicating that physical layout also affected signal propagation.

Band Performance

Higher frequency bands were capable of providing high throughput when propagation conditions were favourable. However, performance at the higher frequency bands degraded significantly in the most challenging high-density environment.

The results indicate that frequency alone does not determine performance in a complex indoor environment. Crowd density, physical layout and obstruction also influence which band is most suitable.

UDP vs TCP

UDP testing revealed significant packet loss under heavy crowd density that was not apparent in the same way in TCP results.

At LV3-03, both 2.4 GHz UDP and 6 GHz UDP experienced approximately 55% packet loss, while TCP primarily showed reduced throughput as conditions deteriorated.

Multi-Link Operation (MLO)

MLO demonstrated a notable advantage under heavy crowd density conditions, recording an average RSSI of -34.64 dBm at LV3-03.

This was substantially stronger than the single-band 5 GHz and 6 GHz UDP measurements at the same location. However, MLO throughput remained relatively modest, suggesting improved signal reliability did not necessarily result in maximum throughput.

Hypothesis

Null Hypothesis

The null hypothesis entering this study was that higher frequency bands would deliver superior performance across all mall conditions due to their greater bandwidth capacity.

The findings partially reject this hypothesis. While 6 GHz did achieve the highest throughput under favourable conditions, its performance degraded significantly under heavy crowd density.

2.4 GHz demonstrated greater resilience to obstruction and crowd density, indicating that frequency alone does not determine performance in complex indoor environments. Instead, the relationship between frequency, crowd density and physical layout influences which band is most appropriate for a given deployment scenario.

Propagation Model Analysis

Best-Fit Model

The measured RSSI values were compared with theoretical propagation models to determine which model best represented WiFi 7 signal behaviour within the mall environment.

Using the combined available testing data, the fitted Log-Distance model produced an estimated path-loss exponent of approximately 1.52.

Model MAE RMSE
Free-Space Baseline 4.16 dB 5.08 dB
Fitted Log-Distance 3.85 dB 4.99 dB

The fitted Log-Distance model therefore produced a slightly lower overall prediction error than the Free-Space baseline.

Log-Normal Shadowing Model

The Log-Normal Shadowing model uses the Log-Distance model as its average propagation trend while also accounting for random variations around that trend.

For the combined Westfield measurements, residual variation around the fitted Log-Distance prediction was approximately 5 dB. This variation was consistent with the changing mall environment, where walls, people, shop fronts and changing line-of-sight conditions caused signal strength to fluctuate independently of distance.

Therefore, the Log-Normal Shadowing Model was considered the most appropriate overall model for representing the Westfield environment, while the fitted Log-Distance model provided the underlying average RSSI prediction.

Conclusion

This project investigated WiFi 7 link quality in a high-density shopping mall environment using the 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz and 6 GHz frequency bands.

The results showed noticeable differences between testing locations. Level 3 experienced the strongest signal degradation, particularly at the higher frequency bands, while other locations produced more stable RSSI values.

This indicates that distance alone does not fully explain WiFi 7 signal behaviour in a shopping mall. Factors such as people, walls, shop fronts, reflections, multipath propagation and changing line-of-sight conditions also affected measured signal strength.

The propagation model comparison showed that the fitted Log-Distance model produced slightly lower prediction error than the Free-Space baseline. However, the Log-Normal Shadowing Model was considered the most suitable overall representation of the Westfield environment because it accounts for both distance-related path loss and additional variation caused by obstacles and changing indoor conditions.

Overall, the findings demonstrate that real-world WiFi 7 performance in a shopping mall differs from ideal theoretical conditions. The collected measurements and best-fit model analysis can support the development of a practical WiFi 7 link-quality estimation tool.
